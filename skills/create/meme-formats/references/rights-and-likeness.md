# Rights, likeness and platform rules for memes (checked 2026-10)

Read this in step 3, before making anything that borrows a picture, a character, a sound or a person. This is
orientation for creators, not legal advice; when money, a brand deal or a takedown is involved, ask a lawyer.

## Copyright: the picture is usually owned

- Famous meme frames are photos, film and TV stills, comics and artworks with owners. Some are stock photos sold under
  licence. Personal accounts often get away with sharing them; **brand and creator accounts that earn money are the
  ones that get claims**, because the use is commercial.
- Meme characters are licensed IP too. In January 2018 a US jury awarded Grumpy Cat Limited $710,001 against a coffee
  company that used the cat beyond what its licence allowed (copyright and trademark).
- The defences are narrow and local: US fair use is decided case by case (commercial use weighs against it); the UK has
  a "caricature, parody or pastiche" exception (CDPA s30A) that still requires fair dealing; the EU's DSM Directive
  (Art. 17(7)) protects quotation, criticism, review, caricature, parody and pastiche when users upload to large
  platforms. None of them is a licence to reuse a frame in a sponsored post.
- **House rule:** rebuild the structure with your own generated images. Use someone else's image only with a licence
  you can show (keep it in the prompt library) or through the platform's own in-app tools (Stitch, Duet, Remix,
  in-app GIF and sticker libraries), which stay inside that app and its terms.
- Sounds: use the platform's sound library inside the app. Business accounts on Instagram are limited to the Meta Sound
  Collection (see [music-and-sound-for-shorts](../../music-and-sound-for-shorts/SKILL.md)).

## Likeness: no real people

- Using a real person's face, voice or name to sell or promote something can break publicity and personality rights
  (most US states; Tennessee's ELVIS Act, in force since 1 July 2024, covers AI copies of voice and likeness).
- The EU AI Act Article 50(4) (applies from 2 August 2026) requires deployers to disclose deepfakes: content that
  resembles real people, places or events and would falsely appear authentic. A satirical work gets a lighter duty, but
  it still has to be disclosed.
- Platforms: TikTok's guidelines (revised September 2025) bar AI content that is misleading about matters of public
  importance or harmful to individuals; Meta and YouTube require labels on realistic synthetic content; X labels or
  removes deceptively altered media likely to cause harm. Details:
  [ai-disclosure-and-provenance](../../ai-disclosure-and-provenance/references/platform-rules.md).
- **House rule (matches Claudia's never list):** no real people in generated memes, not as a face, a voice, a name in a
  fake quote, or a "looks like" prompt. Public figures included. Other creators included, even fans, unless they asked
  to collaborate in writing.

## Trademarks

- Real logos, products and brand mascots in a meme can read as endorsement or ridicule of that brand. Generate "no
  readable logos", and don't make a brand the butt of a joke on a monetised account.
- Platform logos and interfaces: don't fake them (fake app screenshots read as real; see
  [quote-cards-and-text-posts](../../quote-cards-and-text-posts/SKILL.md) step 7).

## Format origins

Some formats and symbols started in, or were taken over by, hate communities. Before joining an unfamiliar format, look
up its origin and current use (Know Your Meme for history; the ADL Hate Symbols database for symbols, numbers and
phrases). If it is contested, skip it: there are always other jokes.

## Money and coins

- No "to the moon", "wagmi", "next 100x" or chart-goes-up memes about any coin. A joke price call is still a price
  call ([crypto-marketing-compliance](../../../grow/crypto-marketing-compliance/SKILL.md)). Any coin mention ends with
  "not financial advice"; TikTok removes crypto promotion entirely.
- A brand paid for the meme, gave you something, or you earn on a link: `#ad` and the platform's paid-partnership
  label, on a meme like any other post.
