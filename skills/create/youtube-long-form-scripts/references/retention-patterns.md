# Retention patterns for 6–15 minute videos

Read this before outlining (step 3) and when reading a retention graph (step 9). These are craft patterns, not
platform rules; test them against your own analytics.

## Where viewers leave, and why

| Where | What the graph shows | Usual cause | Fix in the next script |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | steep drop | the opening doesn't match the title or thumbnail | open on the thing the thumbnail shows |
| 10–60 s | steady slide | setup, context or a long intro before anything happens | cut to the first payoff by 0:30 |
| A cliff mid-video | sudden drop | a beat that broke the promise: a tangent, a sponsor read without a bridge, a slow demo | move it, shorten it, or bridge into it with a question |
| A bump | line rises | people rewatched: a reveal, a dense diagram, a joke | more of that, earlier |
| Last 10–20% | drop | the video signalled it was over ("so to wrap up") | end on the last payoff; recap in one line; end screen |

YouTube Studio's audience retention report (Analytics → Engagement) shows the curve and, on many videos, key moments
such as the intro, spikes and dips. Compare a new video with your channel's typical curve, not with other channels.

## Hook types that fit long-form

1. **Show the end first.** "This is the account after 30 days. Here's what I changed." Works for builds and experiments.
2. **The problem on screen.** The broken thing, the bad result, the confusing chart, then "here's why".
3. **The specific question.** "Why do my agents' best posts all go out after midnight?"
4. **The contrarian line.** "Posting more made it worse. Here's the data." Only if the video proves it.
5. **The stakes.** What the viewer loses by not knowing, in one sentence. Never fear or money promises.

Avoid: "in this video I will…", a logo sting before the first line, asking for a subscribe before any value.

## Re-hooks (every 60–90 seconds)

| Re-hook | Line shape |
|---|---|
| Open loop | "the third one surprised me, and it's the one you can fix today" (and then pay it off) |
| Reveal | "here's what the numbers actually said" + the chart |
| Shift | a new location, a new format (screen to camera), a new voice |
| Countdown | "two left" |
| Mini-cliffhanger at a chapter end | the next chapter's question, asked before the cut |

Every open loop must close. Unclosed loops feel like bait and show up as comments complaining.

## Pacing rules

- A visual change every 4–8 seconds in explainers (cut, zoom, B-roll, graphic); slower in story videos.
- One idea per sentence; say the number, then show it.
- "But" and "so" connect beats; "and then" lists them. Lists lose people.
- Put the second-best chapter first and the best chapter second; the first chapter proves the video is worth it, the
  second rewards staying.
- Sponsor reads: after the first payoff, bridged with a line that connects to the topic, disclosed out loud.

## Endings

- The last payoff, then one line of recap, then the end screen pointing to one specific next video ("the one where the
  agent got it wrong"). Not "check out my other videos".
- No "thanks for watching, see you next time" monologue: viewers leave at the first signal that it's over.

## Story structure for character videos (Claudia or your own)

- Want → obstacle → attempt → result → what changed. A character wants something specific ("a week of posts without
  touching a button"), not "to talk about agents".
- The character stays in voice: Claudia is warm, dry and specific, and is open about being an AI character. She never
  claims to be human and never gives financial advice.
