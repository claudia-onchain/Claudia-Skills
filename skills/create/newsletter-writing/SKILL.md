# Newsletter writing

A newsletter is the one channel where the audience asked to hear from you, and nothing ranks it except whether they
open and read. This skill writes one issue with one job, a subject line that tells the truth, a body built from the
week's best material rather than a pile of reposts, links that work and are tagged, and the labels an AI-made or
sponsored issue needs. A person reads the test send and approves before it goes out.

## When to use this

- Writing this week's (or this month's) issue for a creator, an AI character such as Claudia, or an agent.
- Turning a week of posts, threads and clips into an email that is worth opening on its own.
- A launch, milestone or recap email, or an issue with a sponsor or affiliate link.
- An issue is drafted and needs a subject line, a link check or a disclosure check.

Not for: growing the list, lead magnets, referral programmes or welcome sequences (see
[newsletter-growth](../../grow/newsletter-growth/SKILL.md) and
[email-and-dm-sequences](../../grow/email-and-dm-sequences/SKILL.md)), or buying lists and emailing people who never
opted in.

## What you need

- The newsletter platform the operator already uses (Substack, beehiiv, Kit, Ghost or similar), with a test-send
  address. The platform sends; this skill writes.
- The voice guide ([brand-voice-guidelines](../../grow/brand-voice-guidelines/SKILL.md)); for Claudia, her
  [voice reference](../claudia-character-bible/references/voice-and-personality.md).
- Last period's posts with their numbers (saves, replies, shares, follows), and any notes, launches or questions from
  readers.
- Sponsor or affiliate details if any: who paid, what was given, the exact link.
- [references/email-rules.md](references/email-rules.md) — CAN-SPAM, Gmail sender rules, disclosure and crypto
  wording (checked 2026-10). Read it before the first issue and when anything about sending changes.

## Steps

1. **Give the issue one job.** Write it in one line before anything else: "teach one thing", "recap the week",
   "announce the launch", "tell one story". Pick the format that fits:

   | Format | Main piece | Works for |
   |---|---|---|
   | Recap | 3–5 short items, each with why it mattered | Busy weeks, AI characters with many posts |
   | Deep dive | One 600–1,200 word piece plus 2 short items | A topic your posts only touched |
   | How-to | Numbered steps with one example | Questions readers asked twice |
   | Story | One scene, one lesson | Milestones, behind the scenes |
   | Launch | What, why, how to try it, what it is not | Products, skills, coins (news only) |

2. **Mine the week, don't paste it.** Pull the top 3 posts by saves, replies or follows, plus any reader question.
   For each, write what the post couldn't fit: the why, the numbers, the example, what you'd do differently. A
   screenshot of a post is not a section. Method for turning posts into long form:
   [content-repurposing](../content-repurposing/SKILL.md).

3. **Outline on the template.** Copy [templates/issue-template.md](templates/issue-template.md): subject, preheader,
   opener (2–3 lines that earn the scroll), main piece, short items, one call to action, sign-off, footer. Cut any
   section that doesn't serve the job from step 1.

4. **Write the subject line and preheader.** Draft five subjects, pick one, keep one as a test variant if the platform
   supports A/B tests. Rules:
   - Specific beats clever: "41 videos, 6 did 80% of the views" beats "Some thoughts on content".
   - Put the meaning in the first few words; phone inboxes cut long subjects.
   - It must match what is inside: deceptive subject lines break CAN-SPAM. Never fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
   - No price, ticker hype, all caps or urgency for a coin. A preheader adds to the subject; it doesn't repeat it.
   Formulas and 30 examples: [templates/subject-line-bank.md](templates/subject-line-bank.md).

5. **Write the body in the voice.** Short paragraphs (1–3 sentences), a heading per section, one idea per section,
   the most useful thing first. Write for a reader with images off: every image has alt text and the point is in the
   words. Aim for a 3–6 minute read and cut the rest. Read it aloud once; anything you wouldn't say, rewrite.

6. **Clean every link.** For each link:
   - Full `https://` URL to the real destination; descriptive link text ("the calendar template"), never "click here".
   - UTM tags so clicks show up by issue: `?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=<issue-slug>`.
   - Open it once from the test send. Dead links are the most common avoidable mistake.
   - Affiliate links say so next to the link. No links that buy a coin; coin pages and contract addresses only, as news.
   - One clear call to action. Secondary links are fine; a second big button is not.

7. **Add the labels.** Paste the footer and labels from the template:
   - AI disclosure, in plain words, if any of the issue was AI-generated: "Written by <character>, an AI character;
     edited and sent by <operator>." For Claudia: "AI-generated, reviewed by a human."
   - Sponsored sections start with "Sponsored by <brand>" or "#ad" and say what was paid or given.
   - Any coin, ticker or contract address: news or education only, with "Not financial advice."
   - Footer: who is sending, why the reader gets this, a working unsubscribe link and a valid postal address
     ([references/email-rules.md](references/email-rules.md)).

8. **Test-send, then a person approves.** Send to yourself and read it on a phone and a desktop, in light and dark
   mode, once with images off. Check the [templates/send-checklist.md](templates/send-checklist.md) line by line.
   The operator approves and schedules; an agent drafts but never presses send.

9. **Teaser out, numbers in.** After sending, post one teaser per network that stands on its own and links to the web
   version, drafted and approved through `@useclaudia/social` (preview first). A week later, log clicks per link,
   replies and unsubscribes. Treat open rates as rough (mail apps preload images); replies and clicks say more. Feed
   the best-clicked topic into the next issue.

Quick fill-in:

```text
JOB: <one line>
SUBJECT: <specific, front-loaded, true> · PREHEADER: <adds, doesn't repeat>
OPENER: <2–3 lines>
MAIN: <heading> — <the piece>
SHORT ITEMS: <1–4, each 2–4 lines with one link>
CTA: <one action, one link with UTM>
LABELS: AI <yes/no + line> · SPONSOR <none | who + what> · NFA <if a coin is named>
APPROVED BY: <name> · SEND: <date time tz>
```

## Check before you finish

- [ ] The issue has one job and every section serves it.
- [ ] Subject line matches the content; no fake "Re:"/"Fwd:", no coin hype.
- [ ] Every link opened from the test send, tagged with UTMs, with descriptive text.
- [ ] AI disclosure present if any part was AI-generated; sponsors and affiliate links labelled.
- [ ] Coin mentions are news or education with "Not financial advice."; no buy links, no price talk.
- [ ] Footer has the sender, why they get this, a working unsubscribe link and a postal address.
- [ ] Read on phone and desktop, light and dark, images off; a person approved the send.

## Pitfalls

- **The link dump.** Twelve links and no point. One job, one call to action.
- **Pasting posts.** Readers already saw them. Add what the post couldn't hold, or leave it out.
- **Clickbait subjects.** High opens, then unsubscribes and spam reports, which hurt every later send.
- **"AI-assisted" fog.** Say plainly what was AI-generated and who reviewed it.
- **Hidden sponsorships.** An unlabelled paid mention breaks FTC rules and the readers' trust at once.
- **Images carrying the message.** Many readers see a blank box first. Put the point in the text.
- **An agent pressing send.** Email can't be unsent. Drafts only; a person sends.
