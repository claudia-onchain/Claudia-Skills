# Disclosure for affiliate and ambassador posts (checked 2026-10)

Read before sending the affiliate kit (step 5) and before posting any affiliate link yourself. A practical summary,
not legal advice.

## The rule in one line

Any commission, fee, free product or perk is a material connection. Say so in plain words, where the link or mention
is, before the viewer has to click "more".

## United States: FTC

- **Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255, revised 2023):** material connections include money and free or discounted
  products, and the FTC's FAQ says affiliate-link commissions must be disclosed. Disclosures must be clear and
  conspicuous; a platform's built-in label alone may not be enough.
  https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking
- **Wording the FTC accepts** ("Disclosures 101"): "ad", "advertisement", "sponsored", `#ad`, `#sponsored`,
  "<Brand>Partner", "<Brand>Ambassador". Avoid "sp", "spon", "collab", and a bare `#affiliate` buried in hashtags.
- **Placement:** next to the link or recommendation; in video, said and shown; on a livestream, repeated.
- **Brands running programs** should tell affiliates to disclose and monitor that they do.

## United Kingdom: ASA / CAP and CMA

- The ASA's influencer guide treats affiliate links as ads; label them "Ad" or `#ad` up front. "Sponsored" and "Gifted"
  alone are on its list of labels to avoid. https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html
- An ambassador relationship (ongoing payment or perks) makes related posts ads even when a specific post wasn't paid.
- The CMA can fine directly under the DMCC Act 2024 (up to 10% of global turnover, since April 2025).

## Program-specific wording

- **Amazon Associates:** the Operating Agreement requires "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases",
  shown clearly and prominently (Operating Agreement, updated October 2025).
  https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement
- Other programs often have their own required line; copy it exactly from their terms.

## Platform labels

| Platform | Label | Applies to affiliates? |
|---|---|---|
| X | "Paid Partnership" in Content Disclosure settings | yes: X's policy counts gifts, affiliate commissions and ambassador deals |
| Instagram / Facebook | "Paid partnership" (tag the brand) | yes for brand relationships; add "Ad" text for affiliate links too |
| TikTok | "Disclose commercial content" → "Branded content" (or "Your brand" for your own product) | yes |
| YouTube | Studio: "My video contains paid promotion…" | yes, plus the text line in the description next to links |

## AI-specific points

- If the post's images, video or voice are AI-generated, the AI label goes on as well. `#ad` and an AI label cover
  different things; one never replaces the other.
- An AI character can't have personal experience of a product. The FTC's 2023 update covers virtual influencers as
  endorsers. Write what the product is and does (from the seller's substantiated claims), not "I love it".
- The ASA (May 2025): saying an influencer is AI-generated can help avoid a misleading impression, but won't fix a
  misleading claim.

## Lines to copy

```text
Ad · affiliate link: I earn a commission if you buy through this link.
Ad · I'm a paid <Brand> ambassador. <One true sentence.>
As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.
Ad · affiliate link. This scene is AI-generated; the product is real: <link>
```
