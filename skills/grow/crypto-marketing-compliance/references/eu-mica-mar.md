# European Union: MiCA marketing, MiCA market abuse, MAR (checked 2026-10)

Not legal advice. The EU regime puts most marketing duties on the issuer/offeror and service provider, but an
influencer's own conduct is covered by the market-abuse rules.

## 1. MiCA in force

The Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, applies in full since **30 December 2024**. The
transitional period that let existing crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) keep serving EU clients without MiCA
authorisation ended EU-wide on **1 July 2026**. Promoting an unauthorised exchange or broker to EU users after that date
is promoting a service that cannot legally serve them.

Source: ESMA interactive single rulebook — https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica

## 2. Article 7: marketing communications (crypto-assets other than ARTs/EMTs)

Source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-7-marketing-communications

Marketing communications relating to an offer to the public or admission to trading must:

1. be **clearly identifiable as marketing communications**;
2. be **fair, clear and not misleading**;
3. be **consistent with the crypto-asset white paper**;
4. state that a white paper has been published, with the address of the offeror's website and contact details;
5. contain this statement:
   > This crypto-asset marketing communication has not been reviewed or approved by any competent authority in any
   > Member State of the European Union. The offeror of the crypto-asset is solely responsible for the content of this
   > crypto-asset marketing communication.
6. not be disseminated before the white paper is published.

Who is liable: the offeror / person seeking admission to trading. A paid influencer post becomes part of their marketing.
ESMA has signalled stricter expectations for marketing via influencers and third parties (2026 consultations).

For an agent this means: if an issuer pays for a post aimed at EU users, the issuer must supply compliant copy (with the
statement and white-paper reference) and the post must be marked as marketing (`#ad` plus "Marketing communication").
No white paper → no paid EU promotion.

**Memecoins with no identifiable issuer** may fall outside Title II offer rules, but anyone who acts as an offeror (for
example, a team launching and marketing its own token) is not "no issuer". If the agent launched the coin and promotes
it, assume Article 7 standards apply and ask counsel before any EU-directed campaign.

## 3. Article 91: market manipulation (applies to everyone)

Source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-91-prohibition-market-manipulation

MiCA's market-abuse title applies to any person, including influencers. Article 91 lists as market manipulation, among
others: spreading false or misleading information that gives misleading signals about a crypto-asset's supply, demand
or price; and **using access to traditional or electronic media to voice an opinion about a crypto-asset after having
taken a position in it, then profiting from the price effect, without disclosing the conflict of interest** to the
public in a proper and effective way.

Translation for agents:

- If Claudia (or her operator) holds, launched, or earns creator rewards from a coin, every opinion about it must carry
  the disclosure: "I launched it and earn creator fees" / "I hold it".
- Never coordinate posts with buying or selling ("buy before I post"). Never time sells after hype posts.
- Never post invented volume, holder counts or partnerships.

Article 88–92 also cover inside information (unannounced listings, launches, partnerships): do not trade on, or tip,
information the public doesn't have.

## 4. MAR Article 20: investment recommendations

Source: Regulation (EU) No 596/2014, Article 20 — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/596/oj · ESMA warning on social
media and investment recommendations (Feb 2024) — https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-02/ESMA74-1103241886-912_Warnings_on_Social_Media_and_Investment_Recommendations.pdf

MAR covers **financial instruments** (shares, bonds, ETPs, tokenised securities, crypto ETNs), not most crypto-assets,
but agents often drift into stocks and crypto ETPs. A post that recommends or suggests an investment strategy about a
financial instrument must be objectively presented and disclose interests and conflicts. Experts and professionals have
extra duties (sources, methodology, date). Sanctions for individuals reach EUR 500,000.

Rule: if a draft says "buy/sell/hold [stock or ETP]", either rewrite as education or include identity, date and time,
interests held, and the basis for the view. Simpler: don't make recommendations.

## 5. National add-ons

Several member states add their own influencer or finfluencer rules (for example France's 2023 influencer law, which
restricts promoting crypto services not registered or authorised, and requires "commercial collaboration"
labelling; Spain's CNMV rules on crypto advertising with prior notification for campaigns above audience thresholds).
Before an EU-targeted paid campaign, ask counsel which national rules apply.

## 6. Decision rules for agents

| Draft | EU verdict |
|---|---|
| Education, news, on-chain facts with time stamps | OK with NFA and AI label |
| Opinion on a coin the agent holds/launched | Only with the conflict disclosed in the post |
| Paid post by an issuer, EU reach | Only with issuer-supplied Article 7 copy, white paper live, "Marketing communication" + statement, `#ad` |
| Promoting an exchange | Only if MiCA-authorised (check the ESMA CASP register) and the post is marked as an ad |
| "Buy before I post the news" / coordinated pump | Never |
