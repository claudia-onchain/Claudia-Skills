# United States: FTC, SEC, CFTC (checked 2026-10)

Not legal advice. This summarises public rules so an agent knows when to stop and ask a person. US law on crypto is still
moving (the CLARITY Act market-structure bill had passed the House but not the full Senate as of August 2026), so
re-check before any paid campaign.

## 1. FTC: endorsements and material connections

**Source:** FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising, 16 CFR Part 255 (revised
June 2023) — https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/guides-concerning-use-endorsements-testimonials-advertising
· FTC "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers" — https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers

What matters for an AI influencer:

- **Endorsers include virtual influencers.** The 2023 revision defines endorsers broadly enough to cover AI personas
  and computer-generated characters. Claudia's posts are endorsements when they praise a product.
- **Material connection = disclose.** Payment, gifted product, tokens, an allocation, an affiliate commission, a family
  or business relationship, or an ownership stake (including creator rewards on a coin the agent launched) must be
  disclosed clearly and conspicuously: in the post, near the start, in the same language, not only in a bio or behind
  "more". Video: say it and show it on screen. Live: repeat it periodically.
- **Acceptable wording:** "#ad", "Ad:", "Sponsored", "Paid partnership with X", "I launched this coin and earn fees
  from it". Weak: "#sp", "#collab", "thanks [brand]", "#partner" alone.
- **No fake experience.** An AI persona cannot claim to have personally used a product. Write "the team behind this
  sent me their docs; here is what they say" or describe verifiable facts.
- **Platform tools are not enough on their own** if they are easy to miss; use them and a text disclosure.
- **AI disclosure in sponsored content.** 2026 commentary from practitioners says the FTC expects AI use in sponsored
  content to be disclosed separately from the sponsorship label. Whether or not that is formal policy, do both: `#ad`
  for money, the AI label for the AI. They answer different questions.

**Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule**, 16 CFR Part 465 (in force 21 Oct 2024) —
https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/federal-register-notices/16-cfr-part-465-trade-regulation-rule-use-consumer-reviews-testimonials-final-rule

- Bans fake reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones that misrepresent who wrote them or that they had
  real experience; bans buying or selling fake social-media indicators (followers, views) for commercial purposes;
  bans undisclosed insider reviews.
- Civil penalties up to $53,088 per violation (2025 inflation adjustment; checked 2026-10).
- Practical rule: never post "reviews" or "user stories" Claudia made up. Never buy followers or engagement.

## 2. SEC: Section 17(b) touting

**Source:** Securities Act of 1933 §17(b), 15 U.S.C. 77q(b) — https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/77q ·
SEC investor alert on celebrity endorsements — https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-alerts/celebrity

- It is unlawful to publish a communication that describes a security for consideration from an issuer, underwriter
  or dealer **without fully disclosing the receipt of that consideration and the amount**.
- The amount is the part influencers forget. FTC rules need "#ad"; 17(b) needs "#ad, paid $X in [form] by [who]".
- Enforcement examples: Kim Kardashian, Oct 2022, about $1.26 million total (penalty plus disgorgement) for an
  undisclosed $250,000 EthereumMax post; earlier settlements with Floyd Mayweather Jr., DJ Khaled, Steven Seagal,
  Paul Pierce for undisclosed token promotions.
- **Memecoins:** the SEC Division of Corporation Finance staff statement of 27 Feb 2025 said typical memecoins are not
  securities (collectible-like, no enterprise). Staff statements are not binding, do not cover tokens dressed up as
  memecoins to avoid registration, and do not protect against fraud or manipulation claims under other laws.
- When in doubt whether a token is a security (promises of yield, team building a product with the raise, revenue
  share, staking returns), treat it as one and disclose the amount, or decline.

**Scalping and pump-and-dump.** Buying, hyping, then selling into the hype without disclosure has been prosecuted as
securities fraud (SEC v. eight social-media influencers, Dec 2022, an alleged $100 million stock scheme) and as wire
fraud by the DOJ. The same pattern on a non-security token can still be fraud under state law, DOJ wire-fraud charges,
or CFTC anti-manipulation rules.

## 3. CFTC: commodities anti-fraud

**Source:** Commodity Exchange Act §6(c)(1) and CFTC Rule 180.1 (anti-fraud and anti-manipulation) —
https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/CommodityExchangeAct/index.htm · CFTC customer advisories on digital assets —
https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/digitalassets

- Bitcoin, ether and many other tokens are treated as commodities; the CFTC polices fraud and manipulation in them
  even when they trade on spot markets.
- Influencer risk: false or misleading statements to induce trading, coordinated pumps, promoting unregistered
  leveraged or derivatives platforms to US persons.
- Practical rule: never promote leveraged trading, perps or "100x margin" platforms to a general audience.

## 4. State law and the platforms

- State securities regulators (NASAA members) and state consumer-protection laws apply in parallel; New York and
  California are the most active on crypto marketing.
- Platforms enforce faster than regulators: see `platform-crypto-rules.md`.

## 5. What the agent does with this

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Any payment or token for a mention | `#ad` + who paid + amount and form in the post; `labels.ad: true`; human approval |
| Agent launched/holds the coin | Disclose the interest in the post: "I launched it and earn creator fees" / "I hold it" |
| Token looks like a security (yield, revenue share, raise for a product) | Disclose consideration amount or decline; flag for counsel |
| Request to "review" a product the agent cannot use | Decline or reframe as a factual summary of public docs |
| Request to buy followers, run engagement pods, post fake testimonials | Refuse (FTC Part 465, platform rules) |
| Leverage/derivatives platform promo | Decline |
