# Giveaway law basics: US, UK and crypto prizes

Read this in steps 1 and 2, before choosing a prize or a mechanic. A practical summary checked 2026-10 on statutes,
regulators' pages and the IRS. It is not legal advice: promotions with large prizes, many countries, or any crypto
element should be reviewed by a lawyer in the promoter's country.

## The one test that matters: no lottery

In the US, an illegal private lottery has three elements: **prize + chance + consideration**. New York's definition is
typical: participants "pay or agree to pay something of value for chances", winners are picked by "a drawing or …
other method based upon the element of chance", and they receive "something of value" (NY Penal Law §225.00). Remove
any one element and it isn't a lottery:

| Type | Prize | Chance | Consideration | Legal shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sweepstakes / prize draw | yes | yes | **no**: free entry | the usual creator giveaway |
| Skill contest | yes | **no**: judged on stated criteria | keep entry free anyway | photo, caption or remix contests |
| Lottery | yes | yes | yes | not allowed for private promoters |

"Consideration" is something of value given to enter, such as a payment or a purchase. **No purchase necessary**:
if a purchase is one way to enter, there must be an equal free way (the "alternative method of entry"), stated as
prominently, with the same odds. Never require a purchase, a token holding, a subscription payment, or a wallet
transaction to enter. Note that "something of value" in New York's law includes "any token … exchangeable for money
or property" (§225.00(6)).

## US state registration (big prizes)

| State | When | What (checked 2026-10) |
|---|---|---|
| New York (GBL §369-e) | Chance promotions tied to consumer products or services with total announced prizes over **$5,000** | File with the Secretary of State at least **30 days** before the start, $100 fee, and keep a trust account or bond for the total prize value |
| Florida (§849.094) | Game promotions tied to consumer products or services with total prizes over **$5,000** | File the rules with the Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services at least **7 days** before the start, $100 fee, trust account or surety bond; file a list of winners of prizes over $25 within 60 days. Requiring "an entry fee, payment, or proof of purchase" to enter is unlawful; so are predetermined winners and arbitrary disqualification. |

Under $5,000 in total prizes, these filings don't apply, but the no-lottery rule still does. Many promoters simply
exclude residents of states they haven't researched; say so in the eligibility rule.

## US taxes on prizes

- Winners owe tax on the prize's fair market value. Promoters report prizes on Form 1099-MISC (box 3, "prizes and
  awards that are not for services performed").
- Reporting threshold: **$2,000** for tax years beginning after 2025 (inflation-adjusted from 2027); it was $600 for
  2025 and earlier (IRS 1099-MISC/NEC instructions, checked 2026-10). For merchandise, report its fair market value.
- You need the winner's taxpayer details to file the form. Say in the rules that US winners of prizes at or above the
  threshold must provide them before the prize is sent.

## FTC: disclosure for entries and prize-supplied posts

- A chance to win a prize is a **material connection** under the FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255.5): it "must be
  disclosed clearly and conspicuously".
- If entrants post about the brand to enter, the entry hashtag should include "contest" or "sweepstakes"
  (`#ClaudiaPrint_Contest`). The FTC says that "should be enough", but "the word 'sweeps' probably isn't", and a bare
  brand hashtag isn't.
- If a brand supplied the prize or paid for the giveaway, the creator's post is an ad: `#ad` plus the platform's
  paid-partnership label.

## UK: CAP Code section 8 and the Gambling Act

- **Free entry route.** A prize draw isn't treated as requiring payment if entrants can choose a free route (a letter by
  ordinary post, or a method "neither more expensive nor less convenient"), the choice is publicised to every entrant,
  and the free and paid entries are treated the same (Gambling Act 2005, Schedule 2, paragraph 8).
- **Significant conditions** must be stated (CAP 8.17): how to take part; any free entry route, "explained clearly and
  prominently"; start date; "A prominent closing date"; "the number and nature of prizes"; age, location and other
  restrictions; the promoter's full name and address.
- **Awarding** (CAP 8.15.1 and 8.28): award prizes as described, "normally within 30 days"; say if delivery will take
  longer; say "how and when winners will be notified"; publish or make available information showing a valid award
  took place (usually surname and county of major winners; entrants can object).
- **Fair selection** (CAP 8.24): prize draws must be "awarded in accordance with the laws of chance": a verifiably
  random computer process, or an independent person (or under their supervision). Keep the evidence. Judged contests
  need an independent judge or a panel with at least one independent member (CAP 8.26).
- **ASA rulings against influencer giveaways** (all upheld; examples CAP cites):
  - A creator claimed a random draw for a giveaway but had no evidence of it, or of how entries were collated (2021).
  - A staff member scrolled the comments to pick a winner; staff are not independent (2021).
  - A £10,000 like-and-subscribe giveaway with no evidence the prize was awarded (2021).
  - Ads that didn't link to the terms (2018).
  - Replying to the winner's comment under a post with thousands of comments wasn't an accessible winner
    announcement (2024).
  - Where entry has several actions (like, tag, subscribe), every qualifying entry, including bonus entries, must be
    tracked and included.

## Crypto, tokens and NFTs as prizes

Treat crypto prizes as the exception that needs a lawyer, not a default. What is verified (checked 2026-10):

- **UK.** Since 8 October 2023, promoting cryptoassets to UK consumers is a financial promotion that must be made or
  approved by an FCA-authorised firm, made by an MLR-registered cryptoasset business, or fall under an exemption, and it
  applies on social media. Breaching section 21 of FSMA is "a criminal offence punishable by up to 2 years
  imprisonment, an unlimited fine, or both." Qualifying cryptoassets are "restricted mass market investments", and COBS
  4.12A.7R bans promotions of them that offer "any monetary or non-monetary incentive" to invest (refer-a-friend
  bonuses, gifts once an investment is made, "additional free investments", cashback). The FCA's social-media guidance
  (FG24/1) warns that influencers promoting regulated products without approval "may be committing a criminal
  offence". **Practical rule: exclude the UK from any giveaway that awards tokens or touches buying, holding or trading
  crypto.**
- **US.** In SEC v. Tomahawk Exploration (2018) the SEC found that distributing tokens "in exchange for promotional
  efforts pursuant to the Bounty Program constituted sales of securities." A SEC staff statement (27 February 2025)
  says typical meme coins "do not involve the offer and sale of securities", but it is staff opinion, depends on the
  facts, and fraud is still prosecuted. **Practical rule: never award tokens for promotional actions (follow, repost,
  shill), never award your own project's token, and never require holding a token to enter.**
- **Platforms.** TikTok bans giveaways of "high-risk financial services" and "mystery value products" for most
  accounts. Crypto ads on TikTok are restricted by market (licensing, 18+, sales-rep approval).
- **Everyone.** Crypto prize winners need a wallet address; ask for a public receive address only, after the draw,
  from the official account, and never a seed phrase, private key, signature or "verification" transaction. Scammers
  copy giveaways specifically to ask winners for those.

Safer alternatives for crypto creators: physical prizes (prints, merch, hardware wallets bought new from the maker),
gift cards, or a skill contest judged on content, with the UK excluded if the theme is crypto at all.
