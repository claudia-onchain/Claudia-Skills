# Platform promotion rules for giveaways and contests

Read this in step 3, before you write the entry mechanic. Checked 2026-10 on each platform's own policy pages. Where an
official page could not be read in full, it says so; re-check those before relying on them.

## Summary: entry mechanics by platform

| Mechanic | Instagram | Facebook Page/Group/Event | X | TikTok | YouTube | Reddit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comment to enter | yes | yes | yes (reply or mention) | yes | yes | with mod approval |
| Like to enter | yes | yes | allowed, but don't reward volume | not banned in an official rule found | yes, but don't manipulate metrics | no (vote manipulation) |
| Tag a friend to enter | risky: no inaccurate tagging | **no**: can't require or incentivise tagging | allowed | not addressed | not addressed | no |
| Share/repost to enter | not in the guideline excerpt read | **no** | discouraged ("whoever Reposts this the most wins") | not addressed | not addressed | no |
| One entry per person | recommended | recommended | **say it**: multiple accounts → ineligible; one entry a day | recommended | recommended | recommended |
| Disclaimer that the platform isn't involved | **required** | **required** (and a release) | not required; follow the X Rules | not found in official rules | **required** (and a release) | **required** |

Safest single mechanic everywhere: **comment once with an answer to a question**. It's genuine engagement, easy to
collect, and no platform bans it.

## Instagram (Instagram Promotion Guidelines)

From help.instagram.com/179379842258600 (partly verified from the official page's excerpt; the page didn't render in
full, so re-check):

- You are responsible for running the promotion lawfully: official rules, offer terms, eligibility (age, residence),
  and any registration a prize needs.
- Each entrant gives a complete release of Instagram.
- The promotion says it is "in no way sponsored, endorsed or administered by, or associated with, Instagram."
- Don't inaccurately tag content or encourage people to, e.g. asking people to tag themselves in a photo they're not in.

## Facebook (Meta Policies for Pages, Groups and Events, item 7 "Promotions")

From facebook.com/policies_center/pages_groups_events (checked 2026-10):

- Comply with applicable law; examples given: provide official rules, disclose offer terms and eligibility, register
  the promotion where required.
- Entrants must "fully release and hold Meta harmless from liability" and acknowledge the promotion is not sponsored,
  endorsed or administered by, or associated with, Meta.
- "Your promotion must not require or incentivize participants to share, repost, tag others, or in any other way
  publicize" the promotion. This replaced the older, narrower "share on your Timeline" rule.
- Meta won't help administer a promotion; using its services to run one is at your own risk.

## X (Guidelines for promotions on X)

From help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/x-contest-rules (read via an archived copy of the official page dated
2025-12-06):

- "Discourage the creation of multiple accounts" and include a rule that "anyone found to use multiple accounts to
  enter will be ineligible."
- "Discourage posting the same post repeatedly"; don't set rules like "whoever Reposts this the most wins"; state that
  multiple entries in a single day won't be accepted.
- "Ask people to mention you in their update so you can see all the entries" (search may not show every post).
- Use a relevant hashtag (for example `#contest`); adding it to unrelated posts can break the X Rules.
- Follow the X Rules; legal compliance is your responsibility.

Automation: an agent may post the announcement through the API like any post, but must not auto-reply to entrants or
DM them unless they asked (see the x-playbook skill). Draw from an exported list, not by scrolling.

## TikTok

From the Community Guidelines (regulated commercial activities, 2026 version) and the Branded Content Policy (effective
31 August 2026), checked 2026-10:

- TikTok's definition of trade includes giveaways: "The sale, purchase, exchange, giveaway, or redirection of goods or
  services." Giving away a regulated item (alcohol, vapes, weapons, and so on) is treated like selling it.
- Not allowed (unless you're a verified business account meeting TikTok's standards): "Gambling, gambling-like
  activities, or mystery value products" and "High-risk financial services". Mystery value products include goods
  "with unknown or variable worth, including money or in-game currency": no mystery boxes, no "random token amount"
  prizes.
- In LIVE: gambling or gambling-like activities are not allowed anywhere; LIVEs that "Trick or pressure people into
  giving Gifts or engaging, like 'like-for-like' schemes or fake incentives" are restricted.
- Branded content: use the content disclosure setting when you receive "any kind of incentive" (for example a brand
  supplying the prize).
- Not found in an official TikTok rule (so not stated as a rule here): a ban on follow/like-to-enter, a required
  "not endorsed by TikTok" line, or a TikTok Shop giveaway cap. Adding the disclaimer anyway costs nothing.

## YouTube (Contest policies and guidelines)

From support.google.com/youtube/answer/1620498 (checked 2026-10):

- You are "solely responsible for your contest", which must follow applicable law, including US sanctions.
- Entry must be "free of charge"; no contests run "through ad units".
- Don't "manipulate metrics … to misrepresent genuine viewer engagement" (no "sub + like 10 times to enter").
- Official Rules must: link to the Community Guidelines and say non-compliant entries are disqualified; state YouTube
  isn't a sponsor; require entrants to "release YouTube from any liability"; include "a legally compliant privacy
  notice".
- You can't take ownership of entries or require entrants to hand over all rights to them.
- No association of YouTube with the contest without written consent; award prizes as your rules say.

## Reddit

Reddit's help article on contests asks for clear official rules, a statement that the promotion is not sponsored by or
associated with Reddit, no targeting of minors, and no lotteries without Reddit's permission. Most subreddits ban
giveaways; get written moderator approval first.
