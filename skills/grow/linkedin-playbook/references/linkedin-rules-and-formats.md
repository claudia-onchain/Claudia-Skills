# LinkedIn rules, formats and API, for AI brands

Read this before setting up any LinkedIn presence for an AI character, before planning formats, and whenever a post
fails or an automation idea comes up. Everything below was read on LinkedIn's or Microsoft Learn's own pages in
October 2026 (checked 2026-10). Sources at the end.

## Identity: why the character can't have a profile

User Agreement (effective 3 November 2025):

- Section 2.1: "you will only have one LinkedIn account, which must be in your real name". "Creating an account with
  false information is a violation of our terms."
- Section 8.1 (Dos): "Use your real name on your profile".
- Section 8.2 (Don'ts), item 1: do not "create a Member profile for anyone other than yourself (a real person)".

Professional Community Policies: "We require you to use your true identity on LinkedIn" and "We don't allow fake
profiles or entities."

What this means for an AI character:

| Want | Allowed route |
|---|---|
| The character "on LinkedIn" | A LinkedIn Page for the brand or product the character belongs to, run by real admins |
| The character's voice in the feed | Page posts, plus images and clips of the character on the Page, labelled AI |
| Personal reach | The operator's own profile, posting in the operator's own voice about the work |
| Comments as the character | No. Page admins can comment as the Page in the app, by hand, in the brand voice |

## Automation and engagement rules

User Agreement 8.2, Don'ts (quoted):

- Item 2: do not "Develop, support or use software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes ... to
  scrape or copy the Services, including profiles and other data from the Services".
- Item 13: do not "Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts,
  send or redirect messages, create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic
  engagement".

Section 3.6 on AI features: content generated by tools "might be inaccurate, incomplete, delayed, misleading or not
suitable for your purposes. Please review and edit such content before sharing with others", and the member is
responsible for content "regardless of whether it's labeled as created by 'AI'".

Professional Community Policies:

- "Do not spam members or the platform."
- "Don't do things to artificially increase engagement with your content", and "don't agree with others ahead of time
  to like or re-share each other's content" (engagement pods).
- "Do not share synthetic or manipulated media that depicts a person saying something they did not say" without
  "clearly disclosing the fake or altered nature of the material."
- "Do not share content that is false, misleading, or intended to deceive."

LinkedIn's posting help adds that it may limit how many posts a member or Page can make in a period, based on
behaviour, authenticity signals and verification status. It publishes no number.

Safe pattern: the official API posts content a person approved, one post at a time. Comments, reactions, connection
requests and messages stay manual.

## AI labels and Content Credentials

- Images and video "cryptographically signed using C2PA Content Credentials will be noted with the C2PA icon".
  Clicking it shows whether AI was used to generate or edit the content, the app or tool, the creator, the issuer and
  the date. LinkedIn says it is rolling this out gradually and cannot yet label all AI content. In select countries
  uploaders can self-attest that media was made with AI.
- LinkedIn has no AI checkbox in the Posts API. The `@useclaudia/social` LinkedIn connector appends `(AI-generated)`
  to the text (its default text disclosure).
- House rule in this library: every post with an image or clip of the character carries the AI line and
  "AI-generated image:" alt text, and keeps the generator's Content Credentials in the file.

## Formats and limits (LinkedIn help, checked 2026-10)

| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| Post text | 3,000 characters |
| Article (Publishing Platform) | 125,000 characters |
| Images per post | up to 20; 5 MB each; minimum 552 × 276 px, 1080 px wide recommended; ratio 3:1 to 4:5 (multi-photo max 4:5) |
| Link vs image | a post can include a URL link or an image, not both |
| Alt text | ALT button on desktop and mobile |
| Video | 75 KB to 5 GB; 3 s minimum on desktop (2 s on mobile), 15 min maximum; aspect 1:2.4 to 2.4:1; MP4, MOV, WEBM and others |
| Page video | 10 min maximum (Pages and Career Pages spec) |
| Documents | PPT, PPTX, DOC, DOCX, PDF; up to 100 MB and 300 pages; videos inside show as static images |
| Polls | question 140 characters; 2–4 options of 30 characters; default duration one week (LinkedIn's launch post: 24 hours to two weeks); duration can't be changed after posting |

`@useclaudia/social` LinkedIn limits: 3,000 characters, 1–20 images or 1 video, no threads, no replies, no inbox; daily
cap 5 posts and 3 link posts per account (package defaults).

## Feed ranking: what LinkedIn says

LinkedIn's help page "LinkedIn relevance" (checked 2026-10) names three signal groups:

- **Identity:** location, workplace, skills of the viewer.
- **Content:** "How often a post is viewed or engaged with" and "How constructive or professional the conversation is."
- **Activity:** "Content you spend the most time viewing" and "What you've reacted to, commented on, or shared."

It adds that algorithms "filter out or taper distribution of low-quality and unsafe content", and that feed
distribution "isn't influenced by payments from third parties", apart from labelled promoted content. LinkedIn's
engineering blog has described dwell time (time spent on a post) as a ranking input since 2020.

LinkedIn does not publish a link penalty, an ideal post length or "golden hour" numbers. Treat those claims in
third-party guides as untested until your own analytics agree.

## API notes (Microsoft Learn, checked 2026-10)

- Share on LinkedIn grants `w_member_social` ("Required to create a LinkedIn post on behalf of the authenticated
  member"). Throttle: 150 requests per member per day, 100,000 per app per day.
- Posts API (`POST /rest/posts`) needs `Linkedin-Version: YYYYMM` and `X-Restli-Protocol-Version: 2.0.0`. Organic
  content types: text, images, video, documents, article, multi-image, poll. Organic carousels are not supported.
  The package sends `Linkedin-Version: 202609`. LinkedIn sunsets version 202510 on 15 October 2026.
- Posting as a Page uses `w_organization_social`, limited to members with a Page role such as ADMINISTRATOR or
  CONTENT_ADMIN. The package's connector posts as the member only.
- Access tokens last 60 days. Programmatic refresh tokens (365 days) are for approved Marketing Developer Platform
  partners. Self-serve apps reconnect every 60 days.
- Article posts through the API don't scrape the URL: title, description and thumbnail must be supplied. The package
  takes `options.linkedin.linkTitle` and `linkDescription` for this.
