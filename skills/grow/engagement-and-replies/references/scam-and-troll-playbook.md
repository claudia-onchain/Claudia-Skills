# Scam and troll playbook

Crypto-adjacent AI influencers attract two kinds of trouble in the replies: people trying to take money from the agent's
audience, and people trying to get a reaction. Both are handled by a person with the platform's own tools; the agent's job
is to spot them early and never feed them.

## Common scam patterns (2026)

| Pattern | What it looks like | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Lookalike support | `@claudia_supp0rt`: "Having trouble? DM us to fix your wallet" | Report as impersonation, block, hide reply. Pinned warning if repeated. |
| Fake airdrop / claim | "Claudia airdrop live, connect here" + link | Report, hide. Never post the link, even to warn. Describe it ("a fake claim site"). |
| Wallet drainer in "support form" | Google-form or lookalike domain asking for a seed phrase | Report. Warning post: "No real support ever asks for your seed phrase." |
| Giveaway reply hijack | Copy of the agent's avatar replying "Winners DM me" | Report impersonation (X, TikTok and Instagram all have impersonation forms). |
| Pig-butchering / romance DM | Friendly stranger, then "investment opportunity" | No reply; block; never engage. |
| "Pay to be featured" | "Send 0.5 SOL and Claudia will shout you out" (by an impostor) | Report; public line: "I never sell shoutouts in DMs." |
| Fake brand deal | "Brand" asks the agent to install an app, sign a transaction, or pay a fee to receive a contract | Not a brand deal. Handoff to a person; see [brand-deals-and-sponsorships](../../brand-deals-and-sponsorships/SKILL.md). |
| Fake platform notice | "Your account will be suspended, verify here" | Ignore; platforms notify in-app. |

Reporting paths: X impersonation (https://help.x.com/forms/impersonation), TikTok in-app report → Impersonation,
Instagram/Meta impersonation form (https://help.instagram.com/contact/636276399721841), YouTube impersonation
(https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801947). Checked 2026-10.

## The pinned warning post (adapt, keep the four promises)

```text
Quick safety note: I'll never DM you first, never ask for your wallet, seed phrase or a payment, never run giveaways in
replies, and my only accounts are linked from useclaudia.xyz. If an account says otherwise, it isn't me. Report it.
```

The package adds the AI label. No coin names in it, so no NFA line is needed.

## Trolls

- **Don't reply.** Author replies are a strong ranking signal on X; a reply hands the troll reach.
- **Don't quote-post them** to dunk. Same effect, larger.
- **Mute** for annoyance, **block** for repeated targeting, **report** for harassment, hate, threats or doxxing.
- **Restrict / hide reply** where the platform offers it (Instagram Restrict, X Hide reply, TikTok comment filters and
  keyword lists, YouTube "Hide user from channel").
- **Keyword filters**: TikTok and Instagram let the account owner add custom keyword filters for comments. Add slurs,
  scam words ("airdrop", "claim", "support team", "DM me"), and lookalike handles.
- If a troll raises a **fair point** inside the insult, answer the point once, calmly, with a person's approval, and stop.

## Pile-ons and brigades

Signs: sudden spike in replies from accounts that never interacted, repeated phrasing, a quote-post by a large account as
the origin.

1. Pause scheduled posts with the kill switch. A cheerful scheduled post in the middle of a pile-on looks tone-deaf.
2. Don't reply to individuals.
3. Page the operator with the origin post link and a 3-line summary.
4. A person decides: say nothing, one clarifying post, or a correction. Use the holding statements in
   [crisis-and-reputation](../../crisis-and-reputation/SKILL.md).

## Things that look like engagement and aren't allowed

- Replying to strangers based on keywords, hashtags or trending topics (unsolicited automated replies).
- Liking, following, or reposting automatically, including "like everyone who mentions us".
- Engagement pods, reply-for-reply groups, "follow train" threads.
- Buying replies, likes or followers, or paying for "engagement services".
- Running a second account to agree with the first one.

These break X, TikTok, Meta and YouTube rules and, for paid fake engagement, the US FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials
Rule (16 CFR 465, in force October 2024), which bans buying or selling fake social media indicators.
