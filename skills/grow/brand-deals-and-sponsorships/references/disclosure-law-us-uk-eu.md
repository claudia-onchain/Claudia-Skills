# Disclosure law for sponsored posts by AI influencers (US, UK, EU)

Read this in step 6, before writing any sponsored caption, and whenever a brand pushes back on disclosure. This is a
practical summary, checked 2026-10, not legal advice; the operator is responsible for their account and should take
advice for large or unusual deals.

## The two disclosures

| What | Why | How |
|---|---|---|
| **It's an ad** | Consumers must know when content is paid for or incentivised | "Ad" / `#ad` at the start + the platform's paid-partnership tool + (video) said and shown in the first seconds |
| **It's AI** | An AI persona presented as a person, or AI imagery presented as real, is a misleading omission | Native AI label + a plain text line ("Claudia is an AI character; this scene is AI-generated") |

Both, every time. Neither replaces the other.

## United States — FTC

- **Endorsement Guides** (16 CFR Part 255, revised June 2023): an endorsement must reflect the honest opinions,
  findings or experience of the endorser; material connections (payment, free product, family/employment ties,
  affiliate commissions) must be disclosed **clearly and conspicuously** — hard to miss, in the post itself, not only
  in a bio, a "more" fold or a pile of hashtags. The 2023 revision defines endorsers broadly enough to include
  **virtual influencers**. Video: disclose in the video (spoken and on screen), not only in the caption.
  [16 CFR 255 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.0) · [2023 Federal Register notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-07-26/html/2023-14795.htm) ·
  [FTC "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers"](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)
- **Personal experience:** an endorser can only claim experience they actually had. A virtual influencer hasn't
  used a product; first-person usage claims by an AI persona are deceptive. Relay a real person's experience with
  attribution, or describe the product factually from the brand's substantiated claims.
- **Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule** (16 CFR Part 465, in force 21 Oct 2024): bans fake reviews and
  testimonials, including AI-generated ones attributed to people who don't exist or didn't use the product, and bans
  buying or selling fake indicators of social media influence (fake followers, views). Civil penalties up to
  **$53,088 per violation** (2025 inflation adjustment, checked 2026-10).
  [ftc.gov — final rule announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)
- **2026 practice:** compliance commentary in 2026 (e.g. [promise.legal](https://blog.promise.legal/startup-central/ftc-ai-disclosure-rules-creators-2026/))
  reads FTC enforcement as expecting AI use in sponsored content to be disclosed separately from the sponsorship.
  Whether or not that hardens into a rule, doing both costs nothing.
- Wording the FTC accepts: "Ad", "Advertisement", "Sponsored", "Paid partnership with [brand]", "#ad",
  "[Brand] partner". Avoid "#sp", "#collab", "thanks [brand]" alone.

## United Kingdom — ASA/CAP and CMA

- **Who:** the ASA enforces the CAP Code (ads); the CMA enforces consumer law. Joint influencer guidance updated
  January 2026 ([RPC summary](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/winter-2025/cma-and-asa-publish-updated-influencer-guidance-on-social-media-endorsements/);
  [ASA influencer guidance hub](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html)).
- **What counts as an ad:** payment, gifted products or services, affiliate links, discount codes, and the
  influencer's own brand or business. Commercial connection = label it.
- **How:** "Ad" or "#ad" **upfront**, before the "more" cut, in every post and every story frame; platform tools
  (Paid partnership) are good but may not be enough alone if they're not prominent.
- **AI influencers:** CAP's position is that existing rules apply however content is made; disclosing that the
  influencer is AI can prevent a misleading impression, and failing to disclose may be a misleading omission under
  consumer protection law ([RPC on ASA/CAP and AI influencers, 2025](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/summer-2025/asa-and-caps-focus-on-ai-influencer-ad-disclosures/)).
  The ASA has also ruled against filters that exaggerate a product's effect — don't use AI to improve the product.
- **Enforcement teeth:** since 6 April 2025 the DMCC Act 2024 lets the CMA decide breaches and fine directly — up to
  10% of global turnover or £300,000, whichever is higher — and hidden advertising is on its banned-practices list.
  ASA monitoring still finds a large share of influencer ads poorly labelled (checked 2026-10).
- **Financial promotions** (including crypto) are a separate, stricter regime (FCA): see
  [crypto-marketing-compliance](../../crypto-marketing-compliance/SKILL.md).

## European Union

- **Unfair Commercial Practices Directive** (2005/29/EC): hidden advertising ("advertorial" without making the
  payment clear) is a blacklisted practice in all member states. National rules add detail (e.g. France's 2023
  influencer law requires "Publicité" / "Collaboration commerciale" labels and "Image retouchée" / "Image virtuelle"
  labels on retouched or AI-generated images in commercial content).
- **Digital Services Act**: platforms must let users declare commercial communications (that's what the
  paid-partnership toggles are for).
- **EU AI Act** Article 50 transparency duties (disclosing deepfakes and AI-generated content) are scheduled to apply
  from 2 August 2026; check for any later EU changes to the timetable. Labelling realistic AI image, audio and video of
  people as artificially generated satisfies the spirit either way.
- **MiCA**: crypto-asset marketing is its own regime (identifiable as marketing, fair/clear/not misleading,
  consistent with the white paper, mandatory statement). See crypto-marketing-compliance.

## Quick wording table

| Situation | First line |
|---|---|
| Paid post, AI scene | `Ad · Paid partnership with @brand. Claudia is an AI character; this scene is AI-generated.` |
| Gifted product, no fee | `Ad · Gifted by @brand (no fee). AI-generated scene with the real product.` |
| Affiliate link | `Ad · Affiliate link: I earn a commission if you buy. AI-generated image.` |
| Own product (e.g. Claudia merch) | `Ad · My own merch. AI-generated image.` |
| Video | On screen 0–3 s: "AD · AI-GENERATED", spoken: "This is an ad for Northpine, and I'm an AI character." |
