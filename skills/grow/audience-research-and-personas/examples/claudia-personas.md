# Worked example: Claudia's audience research, autumn 2026

Claudia's operator, Dana (they/them), ran one full round over two weeks in September 2026. Numbers are illustrative;
the method and the way the evidence turns into decisions are what to copy.

## 1. Decisions the research had to inform

1. Which of Claudia's pillars to grow: show-the-work, city-life, agents-and-tools, onchain-culture (news and education
   only), community.
2. Whether to start a weekly tutorial series on TikTok and Shorts.
3. What audience description goes in the media kit.

## 4. Survey: 164 answers in 7 days

Posted once on TikTok, Instagram, X and Telegram, pinned for a week; no incentive. Headline answers:

- Came for: learning to make an AI character (46%), the character's look and story (38%), tools for agents (29%)
  (pick up to 2).
- Stage: trying for the first time (39%), doing it regularly (31%), just curious (24%), expert (6%).
- Want more of: step-by-step tutorials (58%), templates to copy (41%), behind the scenes (33%).
- 31 people left an email for a chat.

## 5. Five interviews

Picked for spread: two "trying" creators (TikTok, Instagram), one "doing" agent builder (X), one "expert" developer
(Claudia thread), one "curious" follower (TikTok). What came up in four of five: the hardest part of making a character
is keeping it the same across images; the tutorials they find are either too long or skip the boring parts
(reference packs, rules, approvals).

## 6. Personas

**1. The studio creator** (41% of comments, 46% of survey, 2 interviews)
Wants to make their own AI character that looks the same every time. Stuck on consistency and on what to do between
"nice image" and "a posting account". Says "how do you keep her face the same". Follows when a post teaches one concrete
step; shares tutorials with friends who are starting; leaves when posts are all look and no how.

**2. The agent builder** (22% of comments, 29% of survey, 2 interviews)
Runs or is building an agent and wants it to post and behave well. Stuck on approvals, costs and platform rules. Says
"drop the CLI command", "can my agent post like this". Follows for real commands and honest costs; leaves at hype.

**3. The story follower** (29% of comments, 38% of survey, 1 interview)
Here for Claudia: the look, the worlds, the running jokes. Curious, rarely makes anything. Says "the silver foil set is
everything". Follows for the character; sends clips to friends; leaves if the feed turns into tutorials only.

**Anti-persona: the quick-flip seeker** (6% of comments, 0 survey answers chose it)
Wants price calls and "what's next". Fixed response: "I don't do price calls. Here's how to read a coin's data
yourself", with a link to the rug-check explainer. The agent never writes for this group.

## 8. The persona bet

A four-week test of a tutorial series for the studio creator: "same face, 500 posts", one rule per episode, TikTok and
Shorts, keyword-led titles. Success metric: saves per 1,000 views above Desk at Dusk's 6.1, and fewer "how do you keep
her face the same" questions left unanswered. Persona block given to the agent:

```text
AUDIENCE (from research, 2026-09): write each post for ONE of these:
1. Studio creator: wants their own consistent AI character; stuck on keeping the face the same; says "how do you keep her face the same".
2. Agent builder: wants their agent to post well and safely; stuck on approvals, costs and rules; says "drop the CLI command".
3. Story follower: here for Claudia's look and worlds; says "the silver foil set is everything".
Never write for: the quick-flip seeker (no price talk, no "next 100x").
```
