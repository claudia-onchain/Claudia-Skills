# Disclosure duties for the brand (checked 2026-10)

Read before writing the brief (step 7). The creator posts, but the brand that pays or gifts carries duties too. This is
a practical summary, not legal advice; the creator's side is in
[brand-deals-and-sponsorships](../../brand-deals-and-sponsorships/references/disclosure-law-us-uk-eu.md).

## United States (FTC)

- **Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255 (revised June 2023).** A "material connection" includes payment and free or
  discounted products, whether or not you require a post. Disclosure must be "clear and conspicuous": difficult to miss
  and easy to understand. Relying only on a platform's built-in label may not be enough.
- Acceptable wording per the FTC's "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers": "ad", "advertisement", "sponsored",
  `#ad`, `#sponsored`, "<Brand>Partner", "<Brand>Ambassador". Avoid "sp", "spon", "collab" alone.
  https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers
- The 2023 update says "endorsements" covers virtual influencers too: an AI character can be an endorser, and the same
  rules apply.
- Brands are expected to tell creators what to disclose and to monitor. Keep the brief, your check of each live post
  and any correction requests.
- **Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, 16 CFR Part 465.** Bans fake reviews, including AI-generated reviews by
  people who don't exist, and buying or selling fake followers or views (465.8).
  https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers

## United Kingdom (ASA / CMA)

- The ASA/CAP "Influencers' guide to making clear that ads are ads" (3rd edition, 2023; to be updated for the DMCC Act)
  prefers "Ad" or `#ad`, placed up front. It lists "Sponsored" and "Gifted" among labels to avoid on their own.
  Gifts count as payment in kind even without an obligation to post; affiliate links count as ads.
  https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html
- On AI (ASA, May 2025): no AI-specific code rules; disclosing that an influencer is AI-generated can help prevent a
  misleading impression but won't fix a misleading message.
- Since April 2025 the CMA can decide consumer-law breaches itself and fine up to 10% of global turnover (DMCC Act 2024).
- **Crypto:** promoting qualifying cryptoassets is a regulated financial promotion. Unauthorised influencers who promote
  regulated products may commit a criminal offence (FCA FG24/1). Refer-a-friend and new-joiner bonuses for crypto are
  banned (PS23/6). Don't brief creators on coins.

## European Union

- Unfair Commercial Practices rules require commercial intent to be clear. For crypto, MiCA Article 7 requires
  marketing communications to be clearly identifiable, consistent with a published white paper and to carry the
  "not reviewed or approved by any competent authority" statement; no marketing before the white paper is published.

## Platform tools to name in the brief

| Platform | Tool | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram / Facebook | "Paid partnership" label, tag up to 2 brand partners | Brands can pre-approve creators under Business tools → Branded content |
| Meta ads from creator posts | Partnership ads: creator allows boosting on the tagged post, shares a partnership ad code, or grants account-level permission | Creator can revoke at any time |
| TikTok | "Disclose commercial content" → "Branded content" | Shows "Paid partnership"; required by TikTok's Branded Content Policy |
| YouTube | Studio: "My video contains paid promotion…" checkbox | Shows a disclosure for the first 10 seconds |
| X | "Paid Partnership" label in Content Disclosure settings | Gifts, affiliate commissions and ambassador deals count |

TikTok's market-specific branded content rules (archived October 2025 version) prohibit cryptocurrency and NFTs in
branded content for the US and Canada; the global policy restricts financial services to listed markets
(checked 2026-10). Treat crypto branded content on TikTok as not allowed.

## What the brief must say

1. The exact disclosure line, at the start of the caption and spoken or on screen in video.
2. Which platform tool to switch on.
3. If anything is AI-generated (your character, your visuals), that the post says so, plus the platform's AI label.
4. Claims the creator may make, with your evidence, and claims they must not make.
5. That you will check the live post and ask for fixes, and that missing disclosure means the post comes down.
