# Positioning frameworks for AI influencers and agents

Read the first time you write a positioning statement, and at every quarterly review.

## 1. The statement

```text
For <primary audience> who <want or tension>,
<name> is <category in their words> that <promise>.
Unlike <what they'd follow instead>, <name> <proof only this account can give>.
```

Fill it in this order: audience → alternative → proof → category → promise. Starting from the promise produces
slogans; starting from the alternative produces positions.

### Tests (all must pass)

1. **Stranger test.** Someone who has never seen the account reads the why-follow line and predicts the next three
   posts. If their guesses are wrong, the line or the content is off.
2. **Swap test.** Replace the name with a competitor's. If the statement is still true, the proof isn't specific.
3. **Proof test.** Every claim in "proof" is something the feed already shows, not something it hopes to.
4. **Honesty test.** Nothing in it requires implying the character is human or has real-world experiences.
5. **Platform test.** The promise can be kept on each platform under its rules (TikTok won't allow crypto promotion;
   a promise like "the best calls" fails on day one and is a compliance problem everywhere).

### Weak vs strong

| Weak | Why it fails | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| "A fun, authentic AI influencer sharing her life" | Adjectives, no audience, no proof | "For the AI-curious who want to see what an AI creator's day is really like, Claudia shows the whole thing — labelled" |
| "The future of crypto content" | Category not in the audience's words; implies market calls | "Chart literacy in 30 seconds, for people who keep getting fooled by screenshots" (Juno) |
| "Agents for everyone" | Swap test fails | "The AI that runs its own platform in public: the agents, the launches, the mistakes" |

## 2. Why-follow line

The public, ≤ 12-word compression of the promise. It goes in bio line 1, pinned post line 1, media kit headline.

Formulas:
- **"<identity>, <doing what> <how>."** → "An AI creator, running her own platform out loud."
- **"<outcome> in <format>."** → "Chart literacy in 30 seconds." (Juno)
- **"The <category> that <unexpected verb>."** → "The AI influencer that shows her work."

Avoid: "Welcome to my page", "Just vibes", any number of emoji you'd have to explain, and claims like "the #1".

## 3. Audience definition

| Field | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Who | Age band (always adults for an AI influencer), interests, platform |
| What they want from the feed | Entertainment, a skill, belonging, news, taste |
| Tension | What annoys them about the alternatives |
| Where they gather | Platforms, communities, newsletters |
| Unfollow trigger | What would make them leave (for an AI account: deception, spam, shilling, sameness) |

Primary audience gets ~70 % of content; secondaries share the rest. If the secondary audience is on a different
platform, that's fine: platform split is a good way to serve two audiences without confusing either.

## 4. Character brand vs operator brand

People who run agents have three choices. Pick one deliberately and write it in the guardrails.

| Model | Operator visibility | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backstage | Named only in the disclosure line and on the site ("run by a small team") | Character-led entertainment (Claudia) | Disclosure must still be easy to find |
| Credited | "made by @handle" in bio; occasional operator posts | Builders who want credit and inbound work | Two voices; label operator posts clearly |
| Duo | Operator appears on camera or in threads with the agent | Teaching, behind-the-scenes, trust-heavy niches (Juno + Dana) | Operator's own privacy and safety; consistent cadence |

Whatever the model, the agent's posts must never imply the operator personally endorses something they haven't, and
the operator's posts must say when the agent drafted them if that matters to the claim.

## 5. Category: be first in a small one

AI influencers compete against human creators, other AI characters, and faceless AI channels. Don't claim "AI
influencer" alone: it's a crowded and increasingly penalised category (platforms demote mass-produced AI content —
YouTube's inauthentic content policy, TikTok's originality rules, Instagram's aggregator demotion; checked 2026-10).
Narrow until you can be first:

- AI influencer → AI creator who runs a platform → "the AI that runs her own platform out loud" (Claudia).
- Crypto charts → chart literacy → "30-second chart literacy from an AI that shows its working" (Juno).

## 6. Proof inventory

List the proofs the account can show repeatedly. Each pillar should produce at least one.

| Proof | Format that shows it |
|---|---|
| She runs a real platform | Thread highlights, "what the agents did today", launch-day behind the scenes |
| She makes her own content | Process videos: prompt → generations → the pick → the edit |
| She's consistent | The same face, the same settings, recurring series names |
| She's honest about being AI | Labels, "what's generated in this shot" breakdowns, scripted answers |
| Creators earn on her platform | Plain explanations of creator rewards (70 % of creator fees to the creator on Solana launches), never income promises |

## 7. Positioning maintenance

- Change positioning at most quarterly; change execution weekly.
- Signals that positioning is wrong (not just execution): follows per 1,000 views falling across all pillars;
  comments asking "what is this account?"; the audience you attract isn't the one in the statement.
- Signals that only execution is wrong: one pillar underperforms while others hold; views drop but follow rate holds.
